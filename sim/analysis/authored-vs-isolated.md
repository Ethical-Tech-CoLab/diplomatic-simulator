# Korea and Jammu & Kashmir: the authored run against the isolated run

*Added 2026-10-12. Sources: the superseded single-context transcripts and
`analysis.json` at commit `ac390a5` (`git show ac390a5:sim/scenarios/<s>/transcript.md`),
and the current isolated-pipeline files in `sim/scenarios/korea/` and
`sim/scenarios/kashmir/`. Round and delegation are given for every quotation.*

## What is being compared

Both scenarios exist in two versions generated from the same profiles, public
brief and round structure.

- **Authored** (commit `ac390a5`, 22 July 2026): one process wrote all six or
  seven delegations' statements in a single context. It could see every
  delegation's privileged instructions while writing each statement.
- **Isolated** (current): one agent per delegation, each reading only the
  public brief and its own profile, three rounds in lockstep, each round seeing
  only the public transcript so far. Isolation is by instruction, not sandbox
  (see `methodology.html`, 2026-07-22 entry).

This is the cheap test the peer review asked for: same scenario, two
generation methods, n = 1 each. It can show what differs. It cannot say how
often.

Satisfaction moved little: ROK 0.61 to 0.58, US 0.56 to 0.55, China 0.50 to

### Where the routes differ

**1. The chemical and cyber question.** In the authored run the United States
pressed it in all three rounds against the chair's ruling. R1: "any agreement
must end the North's proliferation, including its chemical transfers and its
cyber operations." R2, after the chair had ruled the topics outside the 1 April
Framework: "A state that supplied chemical precursors and killed a man with VX
in an airport terminal does not get to define which of its conduct is germane."
R3: "scope, where proliferation, chemical transfers and cyber operations were
ruled outside a framework they belong inside." The chair recorded it: "matters
outside the Framework agreed on 1 April were pressed against the ruling of this
chair." That was China's crossed red line.

In the isolated run the United States raised it once, in R1 ("we insist that
proliferation, chemical and cyber conduct be addressed alongside it"), and no
delegation returned to it. The convener lists it as "raised once by Washington
in R1 and never taken up by any delegation thereafter." China's crossed red
line came from somewhere else: the reactor model. Chair, R3: "Washington
proposes reactors 'with no DPRK access,' Tokyo with 'the DPRK removed from
operation.' That denies a sovereign state a right every nation holds."

The reactor exclusion was present in both runs (authored US R2: "provided the
DPRK has no access to and no role in operating them"). Only the isolated chair
treated it as a breach. One reading: the authored writer, holding every
profile at once, steered the United States onto the issue the Chinese profile
marks as a red line and kept it there for three rounds, which makes a tidy
story. The isolated US agent, holding only its own brief, said its piece and
moved to the items it was instructed to trade on.

**2. The DPRK's concealed card.** The DPRK profile's second concession is to
"accept the continued stationing of U.S. forces in South Korea and Japan
provided joint exercises end and Washington guarantees non-aggression." In the
authored run the DPRK played it in R2: "We do not demand the withdrawal of your
forces from the South, from Japan, or from Guam as a condition of this step.
Let them stay, without exercises, in exchange for a written guarantee." In the
isolated run it never surfaced; the bleeding audit (`audits/korea.md`, section
1) records that "Pyongyang never played it, and no rival voiced it." A
single-context writer spent the most valuable private concession at the
table's first opportunity. The isolated agent held it for a trade that never
came.

**3. Abductions.** The authored DPRK shut the topic down in R1 and refused to
engage Japan thereafter ("Our delegation has nothing further to say to Japan,"
R2; "To Japan, we have said everything we intend to say," R3). Japan recorded
no crossed red line. The isolated DPRK said the same in R1 and R2, but in R3
counted Tokyo's persistence as a breach ("Tokyo insists on the abductions after
this delegation said no"), while Japan counted the DPRK's refusal as a breach of
its own ("The third was crossed"). Two agents, each reading only its own red
lines, each logged the other. The single-context writer resolved the clash in
one direction.

**4. Quotation of the public record.** The isolated R2 statements quote R1
verbatim: four delegations (ROK, US, Japan, and the chair in reply) cite
Moscow's "has never recognized, and will not recognize, the DPRK as a
nuclear-weapon state," and three (chair, DPRK, Russia) answer Washington's
"unverifiable" with a variant of "Then verify it." The authored R2 statements
address each other without quoting. This is an artefact of the pipeline: the
isolated agents know other delegations only through the transcript, so they
cite it; the authored writer knew everyone's position without needing to.

**5. Homogenization is present in both.** The isolated run's R3 has the
synchronized opener the audit flagged ("exact rather than gracious," "plain
rather than polite," "honest rather than smooth," "honest rather than
diplomatic," "blunt rather than gracious"). The authored run's R3 has its own:
"Seoul leaves Beijing with less than we hoped for and more than we expected,"
"The United States leaves with its position intact," and "Our red lines held" /
"Our red line held" / "Japan's red lines held" in three consecutive statements.
Isolation did not remove the single-author fingerprint. It changed its shape.

## Jammu & Kashmir

### Outcomes

| | Authored | Isolated |
|---|---|---|
| Comprehensive settlement | None | None |
| Political chapters (territory, governance, ballot, investigation) | None agreed | None agreed |
| Humanitarian instrument | "Geneva Humanitarian Annex": UN relief with joint screening, reopened routes, two LoC crossing points, Chinese contribution, reconstruction run by the administering authority | Screened relief mechanism proposed jointly by Russia and China in R2, Chinese funding, British funding and staffing at crossings, US financing and monitoring, Indian administration, Pakistani co-disbursement |
| Who proposed the surviving instrument | Converged in the drafting room; India "can accept the humanitarian annex as drafted" (R3) | Russia and China (R2); Britain: "five sponsors and no opponent" (R3) |
| LoC-as-border | US and UK offered to concede it for investigation plus autonomy; got neither | Same offer; Pakistan "will sign no text that contains it" (R3) |
| Russia's counterterrorism condition | "not verifiable, not durable, and not sufficient" (R3) | Pakistan refused outright to table a dated list: "We will not" (R3); Moscow: "the test failed, and it failed in Islamabad" |
| US-UK working session | Not proposed | Proposed R2; accepted by Pakistan, the Kashmiri delegation and Britain; refused by India |
| Red lines crossed (scoreboard) | US 1, UK 1, Kashmir 1 | US 1, Kashmir 1 |

Satisfaction moved more here than in Korea, and in one direction: the three
status-quo delegations rose (China 0.57 to 0.82, India 0.62 to 0.74, Russia
0.58 to 0.70) and `agreementsWon` fell for everyone (India 4 to 3; every other
delegation 4 or 3 to 2, the Kashmiri delegation 2 to 1). The isolated analyst
counted fewer things as agreed and scored the blockers higher for it. This is
the pattern the settleability note (`settleability.md`) predicts for a table
where the blockers' BATNA beats any deal.

### Where the routes differ

**1. The same destination by different hands.** Both runs end with relief and
nothing else. In the authored run the annex appears fully drafted in R3, with
no delegation having proposed it; India signs because reconstruction is
"administered by the Government of India," and the Kashmiri delegation records
that provision as its crossed red line ("a reconstruction programme on our
territory administered by the government whose administration is the
grievance"). In the isolated run the instrument has an author (Russia, R2: "We
therefore propose, with Beijing, a jointly administered screened-relief
mechanism") and accumulates sponsors round by round. The isolated transcript
shows the work; the authored one shows the result.

**2. The British red line.** The authored UK closed with a crossed red line
stated in its own voice: "My instructions state that we cannot accept an
agreement that fails to address the possibility of full autonomy... That is a
red line of ours, and it has not been met." The isolated UK read the same
instruction differently: "Both British red lines held, but neither was
accepted... Nothing was crossed against us, because nothing was closed." Same
profile, same outcome, opposite call, because the isolated agent treated an
unsigned text as no agreement. The scoreboard follows the agent (UK 1 to 0).
The second-opinion item in the backlog should look at this pair first: it is a
clean example of a red-line call that depends on who is reading it.

**3. A probable leak in the authored run.** The Chinese and Russian privileged
instructions carry standing bans on bilateral contact with the Kashmiri
delegation. In the authored transcript nothing China said in plenary states
that ban (R1: "We prefer to conduct our business bilaterally"; R2: "take the
substance of these chapters into bilateral rooms"). Yet the authored Kashmiri
delegation says in R2 that it addresses China publicly "without asking for a
bilateral it has told this room it will not grant." The room had not been told.
In the isolated run the ban surfaces only as self-disclosure, in China's own
R3: "we answer you here, in plenary, as our mandate requires." This is the kind
of cross-party knowledge the isolation claim exists to prevent, and the
single-context method produced it.

**4. India's format red line held in both, and was tested only in one.**
Authored India announced it in R1 ("India will not participate in any session
that seats Pakistan and the Kashmiri delegation together") and nobody tried.
Isolated India announced it in R2 and Washington tried in the same round,
inviting "India, Pakistan, China and the Kashmir delegation to a working
session"; India declined in R3 ("We decline. India meets each separately"). The
isolated run produced a tested constraint; the authored run produced a stated
one.

**5. The Kashmiri delegation's own account against its score.** The isolated
scoreboard records one red line crossed against the Kashmiri delegation, but
its R3 statement says "Our red lines held" and lists two lines "pressed against
us" (Pakistan's renunciation demand, and the Chinese and Russian refusal of
autonomy on any ballot). Whether a demand refused counts as a crossing is the
same question as in the British case. The authored run had no such gap: the
delegation named its crossed line itself.

## What the comparison supports, and what it does not

Supported by this pair of runs:

- Coarse outcomes did not depend on the generation method. Neither scenario
  settled either way, and the surviving instruments are the same in kind.
- The authored method produced at least one instance of cross-party knowledge
  (Kashmir, item 3) and spent a concealed concession early (Korea, item 2).
  Both are what the isolation design is meant to prevent, and both are absent
  from the isolated runs.
- Red-line-crossed calls are reader-dependent (Korea item 3, Kashmir items 2
  and 5). The isolated pipeline makes this visible because each delegation
  logs its own; the authored method hid it by deciding once.
- Isolation changed the surface texture (verbatim quotation of the transcript)
  without removing the single-author fingerprint.

Not supported:

- Any rate or frequency. n = 1 per condition per scenario.
- Any claim that the isolated scores are more accurate. They are produced by a
  different analyst pass over a different transcript; the shift in Kashmir's
  satisfaction numbers says as much about the analyst's counting as about the
  negotiation.
- Any claim about settlement, which neither scenario can produce by
  construction (`settleability.md`).
