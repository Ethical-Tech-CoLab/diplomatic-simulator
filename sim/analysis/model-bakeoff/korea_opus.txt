Mr. Chairman, we thank Beijing. China told Washington: you call the freeze "unverifiable"—then verify it. Precisely. We take up the chair's own formulation. The DPRK will accept a monitored freeze at Yongbyon and every declared test site, and readmit IAEA inspectors, the moment financial-institution sanctions are lifted and humanitarian aid flows through WFP and WHO channels—provided Japan-ROK-US exercises are suspended for its duration. Russia offered to press for that access; we welcome Moscow's honest brokerage. To Seoul, who brands our position "never": we accepted your offer to resume inter-Korean dialogue and restore the Comprehensive Military Agreement. That is movement, not "never." But hear our red line clearly—we will not move one step toward dismantlement while sanctions strangle our neediest citizens amid an engineered humanitarian crisis. Peace precedes disarmament, and relief precedes both.
